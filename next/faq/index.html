What Alpha Sentinel is — and isn’t

Each alert gives three things: a sell zone, how far the stock could fall (Target 1 and Target 2), and the stop — the price that says we’re wrong. You decide whether to act. Alpha Sentinel never places trades.

Not for day traders, people looking for tips or buy signals, or anyone who wants their money managed.

The same rules-based screen reads every stock you hold for warning conditions you’d otherwise have to spot yourself.

Targets are where we think the stock could fall — not instructions to repurchase. Alpha Sentinel also doesn’t publish short entries or cover levels. It’s built for people who own the stock.

How alerts work — and their limits

It reads five conditions — trend, momentum, volatility, volume and strength versus the S&P 500 — the same way for every stock. The method is built on 20 years of trading experience. Weights are proprietary; scoring rules are public.

That’s 4:00 AM to 8:00 PM ET on every trading day; overnight sessions are added as the exchanges offer them. We publish our measured delay. Overnight texts are off unless you turn them on; overnight emails still arrive as they happen, and your 5:00 AM email sums up the night. Overnight markets are thinner, and acting overnight needs a broker that offers it.

Stocks can fall without meeting our conditions. Data outages, email filters and carrier delays can stop or slow an alert. If data on one of your stocks is delayed, your dashboard says so — it never shows as clear.
